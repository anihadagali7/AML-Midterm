# Historical research rationale and limits

These notes describe the original experiment sequence. Claims about saved logs, predictions, reports, and sibling-folder layout refer to that earlier workspace. The compact record now retains summaries and reproduction inputs; see README (historical artifact omitted).

This is a separate Part 1 experiment project. It uses only supplied CSVs and the existing eight-repeat grouped folds. Original projects are read-only sources. There is no neural network, pretrained model download, external trial data, ID predictor, test-label search, or automatic Kaggle upload.

## What was ported

The integrated project's `TESTING_POLICY.md` and `RESEARCH_HANDOFF.md` are copied under `references/`. The five saved protocol files under `protocols/` retain exact train157 and pooled235 split assignments and chronological windows. Preprocessing, graph construction, metric learning, pseudo-labeling, vocabulary-free feature construction and calibration see only each fitting partition. Originally masked rows sharing an evaluation embedding group are removed. Evaluation labels are physically dropped from the query frame. Sample-wise F1 is computed on the complete OOF vector per repeat. Repeat SD and wins describe split sensitivity; they are not independent-sample confidence intervals.

The baseline is the **inductive Ani adaptation**, not Ani's transductive submitted model. Its cached predictions were copied and the first fold independently reproduced with exactly identical probabilities. The submitted 70/15/15 ensemble's cached predictions are a second reference. The reported .89423 and .89401 Kaggle scores are historical; this project does not attach either number to a new model.

## New hypotheses and primary sources

1. **Poisson learning:** Calder, Cook, Thorpe and Slepčev, [Poisson Learning: Graph Based Semi-Supervised Learning At Very Low Label Rates](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/calder20a.html), ICML 2020. Their method uses graph sources and sinks to address failures of harmonic label propagation at sparse label rates. Our implementation adapts it to soft multilabel evidence and inductive query extension. It is not an exact reproduction of the paper or its reported datasets. The initial self-scaled graph was disrupted by repeated embeddings. A training-only diagnostic found four connected components before deduplication and one afterward in the first saved fold. The versioned follow-up collapses identical embedding nodes before constructing the graph. This corrects geometry but does not establish that a graph classifier is appropriate here.

2. **Compositional generative model:** Nigam, McCallum, Thrun and Mitchell, [Text Classification from Labeled and Unlabeled Documents using EM](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007692713085), Machine Learning 2000, motivates learning from missing labels through a generative likelihood. Our model is a new adaptation for the supplied continuous vectors: all 31 nonempty reason sets share five semantic prototypes, a shrinkage covariance, anchored EM updates, and fixed gold responsibilities. A matched zero-unlabeled-weight arm distinguishes gains from added data. It assumes that the geometry of combined reasons resembles combinations of single-reason means; that assumption can fail.

3. **Joint F1 decisions:** Waegeman et al., [On the Bayes-Optimality of F-Measure Maximizers](https://jmlr.org/papers/v15/waegeman14a.html), JMLR 2014, explains why optimizing labelwise probability thresholds need not maximize sample F1. With only five reasons, our joint models can enumerate all 31 nonempty predictions and maximize expected sample F1 under their estimated joint distribution. This is optimal only for that estimated distribution, not a guarantee of better held-out F1. The repository already tried other joint decoders; the new contribution is coupling this decision rule to compositional EM or a learned-kernel joint posterior.

4. **Fisher residual features:** Perronnin, Sánchez and Mensink, [Improving the Fisher Kernel for Large-Scale Image Classification](https://www.micc.unifi.it/bagdanov/pdfs/peronnin_etal_ECCV10.pdf), ECCV 2010. Their generative/discriminative representation uses more than mixture membership counts. We adapt mean and optional variance residual features of a training-only diagonal GMM to one reason embedding per trial, then apply signed square-root and L2 normalization. A balanced softmax classifier combines these features with normalized embeddings. The original paper's image results are not evidence that this adaptation will work on clinical trial embeddings.

5. **Recursive feature machines (RFM):** Radhakrishnan, Beaglehole, Pandit and Belkin, [Mechanism of feature learning in deep fully connected networks and kernel machines that recursively learn features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13881), 2023; see also the [authors' repository](https://github.com/aradha/recursive_feature_machines). We implement the classical kernel-machine portion independently in NumPy/SciPy: alternate Laplace-kernel ridge regression and a regularized average-gradient-outer-product update to a 150×150 Mahalanobis metric. No neural layers, backpropagation, or neural training are used. The paper studies both neural and non-neural models; this experiment uses the latter. Square-root spectral shrinkage and averaging gradients over permitted fitting inputs are our explicit regularized adaptations. Kernel derivatives were checked against independent finite differences. The zero-iteration arm is a matched ordinary-kernel control. Follow-ups test duplicate-neutral gradient averaging, grouped bagging, joint output modeling, and a fully nested setting/threshold selection rule.

6. **A useful caution:** Forooghi et al., [Whitening Not Recommended for Classification Tasks in LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.repl4nlp-1.21/), RepL4NLP 2024. Better isotropy or retrieval geometry need not imply better classification. Prior project work already includes whitening and several manifold methods. We therefore did not treat another whitening sweep as a novel architecture or assume that a visually cleaner embedding cloud means better F1.

## Experimental history

The initial 12 recipes were frozen before scoring. The initial graph query calculation underflowed on a fold containing repeated embeddings before any repeat completed. The failed source, registry and log are preserved in `failed_versions/v1/`. Version 1.1 normalizes query weights in log space; this is the same mathematical weighted average evaluated stably. No completed score was dropped.

Version 2 freezes ten recipes before its evaluation: four duplicate-aware graph variants and six learned-kernel/control variants. Version 3 freezes six follow-ups after inspecting version 2. Version 4 freezes two source-prior correction strengths after observing the blend's supplementary rare-label failure; neither passes the CV screen, and neither receives another validation evaluation. Thus these are sequential development studies on reused labels, not 30 pre-registered independent experiments. The saved logs and registries expose every negative result and the order of development. The extension beyond the initial screening gate was explicitly registered as a diagnostic before the extra fits.

The recipe count is not an architecture count. There are four main new model families, with controls and follow-ups. Most prior project architectures remain useful context, but no old fitted model is a component of these candidates. A fixed blend in version 3 contains only the new RFM and Fisher-vector models.

## Course packaging

Unsupervised missing-label generation is explicit in graph/EM models and in the K-means-augmented RFM arm. Pure supervised-kernel and Fisher-representation arms are diagnostic comparisons and do not, by themselves, constitute a complete augmented-data course submission. A future chosen final pipeline must export the generated training labels and satisfy the assignment's complete notebook/report requirements. Do not relabel a diagnostic model as a compliant final submission just because it has a favorable CV score.

## Continued search: versions 5 and 6

The user requested continued experimentation and then asked to add averaging multiple clustering types after the existing work. We completed version 5 first, then version 6. The stronger submitted ensemble became the declared primary comparator before either new screen; Ani remains an exact matched component control. Unlike versions 1–4, some continuation recipes explicitly incorporate historical models to test complementarity. This scope is recorded rather than presented as a wholly new standalone architecture.

**Entropy minimization.** Grandvalet and Bengio, [Semi-supervised Learning by Entropy Minimization](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2004/file/96f2b50b5d3613adf9c27049b2a888c7-Paper.pdf), NeurIPS 2004, motivates preferring confident decisions on unlabeled inputs. We implement classical linear and Nyström kernel softmax classifiers, supervised class balancing, an unlabeled mean-probability tether to discourage collapse, and annealed entropy strengths. Zero-strength arms are controls. The analytic objective gradient is independently checked by central differences. This is neither a neural network nor a claim that low-density boundaries must align with stopping reasons.

**Multiple components within a reason.** We extend the generative EM hypothesis cited above to several mixture components for each reason, with tied shrinkage covariance and anchors to gold-derived initial means. Gold label responsibilities remain fixed at their reason totals; missing labels enter through a fixed total weight. Zero-weight and one-component controls distinguish extra unlabeled likelihood from component flexibility. These are task-specific continuous-vector models, not the multinomial document model from Nigam et al.

**Complementary learned geometry.** The prior RFM predictions showed corrections in Safety/Funding but losses elsewhere. Fixed 15%/30% global and 50% reason-specific uses test that development hypothesis against the submitted ensemble. All component predictions are refitted within each outer fold. The 15% global recipe passes the initial screen but reaches only four repeat wins in each pool after eight repeats. It improves both means and chronology, but reduces standalone validation F1. It is not confirmed.

**Clustering ensembles.** Strehl and Ghosh, [Cluster Ensembles — A Knowledge Reuse Framework for Combining Multiple Partitions](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v3/strehl02a.html), JMLR 2002, motivates combining partition information and co-association similarities. Our four teachers use GMM, k-means, Ward hierarchical clustering, and [HDBSCAN](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.cluster.HDBSCAN.html). We average reason probabilities after fold-local label mapping; raw arbitrary cluster numbers are never averaged. Noise abstains. Separate arms average classifier predictions, require teacher agreement, or form a label-free co-association matrix followed by average-linkage clustering. That last step is an adaptation, not the paper's exact CSPA algorithm. Single-teacher and no-clustering self-training controls preserve the classifier, pseudo-label acceptance rule and decoder. The GMM-only arm reproduces all 80 existing Ani control folds exactly.

The historical `classifier_cross_validation` study already averaged DBSCAN/GMM classifier probabilities. Its modest CV gain lost on validation, using an older protocol. The current experiment tests richer combinations and matched teachers on the current saved grouped folds; it does not claim that averaging was never attempted.

Version 5 freezes 15 recipes and version 6 freezes 16, yielding 930 initial outer fits. One version-5 qualifier receives 50 further outer fits. After version 6 fails the screen against the stronger comparator, a saved diagnostic plan adds 100 outer fits for the three-teacher mean and its exact GMM parent, plus 16 supplementary fits for two candidates and both references. The diagnostic mean gains +.001592 / +.000887 over GMM with 5/8 wins in both pools, but remains below the stronger ensemble and falls on chronology. No promotion gate was changed. Neither diagnostic establishes a leaderboard improvement. All datasets and folds remain reused development evidence.

## Recent-method round: versions 7–10

The recent-method report (historical artifact omitted) records a further 38 recipes with complete source and result artifacts. The three method families were researched from primary papers and author code. Research documents/source code were fetched; no external training data or pretrained weights were downloaded.

- [Gøttcke, Zimek and Campello, Information Systems 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.is.2024.102507): implement Algorithm 1's propagation order with explicit unknown probability mass. This is a journal extension of a 2021 conference method. Our multilabel, duplicate-node and uncertainty-weighted student adaptations are recorded in the registry.
- [Beaglehole et al., xRFM, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10053): implement the classical leaf learner and generalized kernels. The dataset fits in one leaf; no large-data tree partition is needed. Source equations and [author implementation](https://github.com/dmbeaglehole/xRFM) were checked against the independent NumPy derivatives. Inner selection stays inside each outer fit. The fixed one-update follow-up tests the most common inner preference and semantic-vector normalization.
- [Martin et al., CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18437): use the Dirichlet likelihood and MM-quadratic solver with prototype/anchor probability coordinates from supplied embeddings. This is not the full CLIP pipeline and uses no neural encoder. Query inputs remain excluded; only permitted original masked inputs receive EM assignments. The adaptation did not work well here.

The xRFM blends improved both CV means but reached only four pooled repeat wins. Their supplementary evaluations are explicit diagnostic exceptions, frozen before fitting in `recent_diagnostic_plan.json` and `recent_followup_plan.json`. The fixed blend improves chronology and worsens validation. No original confirmation gate was changed and no leaderboard gain is established.

GFTab and UNEM were reviewed but excluded because their full methods require neural training: [GFTab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12864), [UNEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16739). GF-KDA was reviewed, but is not represented as an implemented method. Public source copies retain their licenses and provenance under `references/recent_literature`. The numerical audit includes all four kernel gradients, an independent explicit-unknown-class propagation calculation, SciPy Dirichlet density parity, monotonic M-step checks, fitted-fold replay, query-batch invariance and label rejection.

## Continued iteration: versions 11–13

The continued report (historical artifact omitted) tests 28 variants plus one matched control, totaling 920 outer fits. No variant passed the initial two-pool screen. No new validation/chronological evaluations or test predictions were made. The original five-win confirmation requirement remains unchanged.

This round first incorporates the stronger C.1/LDA 70/15/15 reference from the integration branch. Its eight-repeat .931794/.921631 results are existing work. The adapter reproduces its saved probabilities and decisions on all 80 folds. Previously reported kernel gains used the older .925690/.916489 comparator. Main-repository experiments using different saved pools/folds are separately scoped and cannot be ranked directly against these grouped results.

- Version 11 follows the earlier xRFM work with separate binary-reason gradient metrics, class-balanced gradient averaging and identity regularization. Shared-metric transfer controls distinguish a stronger-parent comparison from a new algorithm. These are our task adaptations, not a claim that the xRFM authors evaluated this multilabel setting.
- [David and Weinshall, VAST, September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14451) motivates version 12. Its graph evidence and adaptive gate are implemented with a linear softmax student replacing the neural training stage. Duplicate-node handling and fractional seeds accommodate the supplied multilabel data. Forward soft-target loss, the paper's explicit reverse KL, propagation removal and a gold-only control are compared. Graph evidence did not improve the student here. This does not reproduce or contradict the paper's neural image benchmark.
- [Rauniyar, Jacobian Aligned Random Forests, December 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08306) motivates version 13. A classical kernel surrogate supplies analytic derivatives; the resulting transform feeds forests or shallow boosted trees. We compare the matrix map with an eigenbasis adaptation, raw/PCA controls and reduced-weight cluster pseudo labels. Transforming features improves some weak tree controls but does not improve the LDA ensemble.

Only research-paper HTML was downloaded in this round; no training datasets, neural encoders or model weights were fetched. [Semi-Supervised Rotation Forest](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocs.2025.102777) was reviewed at abstract level, but its full text returned HTTP 403; it is not claimed as implemented. The [aeon Rotation Forest documentation](https://www.aeon-toolkit.org/en/latest/api_reference/auto_generated/aeon.classification.sklearn.RotationForestClassifier.html) was also consulted. DLPC and meta-confidence ensembles were considered from their papers/abstracts but were not implemented or scored.

Verification independently rescored 184 complete OOF records, checked fold-local feature access and pseudo-label provenance, reproduced the stronger control, verified both KL gradients numerically, and matched graph propagation to an independent literal-loop calculation. The original model/data/protocol/prediction hashes match the starting manifest. Concurrent edits to one benchmark documentation file are explicitly recorded; the audit does not assert that the entire original checkout is byte-identical.

## Focused pairwise follow-up: version 14

Eight further fixed settings test whether the successful LDA component is limited by one shared covariance. Ten pair-specific LDA or logistic heads distinguish each pair of reasons; [Wu, Lin and Weng (JMLR 2004)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v5/wu04a.html) method-2 coupling supplies consistent normalized probabilities. Multilabel positives enter each applicable positive class, and the existing source/ensemble thresholds remain fixed. Compare gold-only and augmented heads, final-only and pseudo-teacher use, and two fixed head weights. This is a focused adaptation of established methods, not another claim of a new publication.

The strongest training-pool result gains +.002831 but loses −.003783 in pooled CV over the same first three repeats. None advances. These 240 additional outer-fold evaluations bring this user-requested continuation to 37 settings and 1,160 outer-fold evaluations, with no new supplementary-label consultation or submission. The pairwise report (historical artifact omitted) retains all eight failures to improve.

The coupling implementation exactly recovers coherent probabilities and agrees with a separate constrained optimizer on inconsistent pair comparisons. Independent artifact checks cover all 48 additional OOF records, fitting boundaries, generated-label provenance and frozen hashes; fitted-fold replay and query-batch/label guards pass. The first 29-setting checkpoint and its executed notebook remain preserved separately.
