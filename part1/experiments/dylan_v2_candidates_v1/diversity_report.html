Which different mechanism is worth pursuing?

Cand1's Correct & Smooth graph mechanism is worth a bounded component experiment against anchored. Cand2 is the closest standalone challenger, but its additional clustering/neighbor features are familiar territory. The graphs in cand1/cand2 supply complementary correct predictions that the overall score hides.

These are the five-seed models on all eight saved Pooled235 repeats. “Fixed” means a completely correct label set where anchored was not completely correct. “Broken” means the reverse. Counts are averages per 235-row repeat, not eight independent datasets.

Cand1 fixes 8 anchored mistakes per repeat, versus 4.5 for the existing LDA ensemble. About 4.75 of cand1's fixes are rows both anchored and LDA get wrong. But cand1 also breaks 14.125 of anchored's correct rows, so replacing or simply averaging entire models is not supported.

For diagnosis only, an all-knowing row selector between anchored and cand1 would score .93651, compared with anchored's .92137 and .92979 for the corresponding anchored/LDA selector. This uses the answers to choose the model; it is an unattainable upper bound without a successful selector, not a prediction of future performance.

The suggested change is in how the 1,014 masked examples and neighborhood structure are used: graph propagation can spread label information and out-of-fold error corrections through connected examples. Anchored currently uses GMM-assisted pseudo-labels and direct nearest-neighbor votes. This remains the same supplied feature information, so large gains are not guaranteed.

The original C&S paper supports the general residual-correction and smoothing mechanism. Its transductive node-classification results do not establish gains for our proposed inductive, multilabel adaptation.

  1. Replace only anchored's pseudo-label generator with the fixed C&S generator. Hold its classifier blend and decision rule fixed.
  2. Separately test C&S-style correction of anchored's grouped inner-OOF errors. Adapt it to independent label scores; do not normalize multilabel scores as if the labels were mutually exclusive. Preserve outer fitting boundaries and the decoder.

Both are proposed follow-ups, not measured improvements. Start with the saved three repeats in both pools and the existing paired advancement rule, then confirm promising changes on all eight. This review did not tune a gate, blend weight or threshold on outer predictions.

Prior research already covers Poisson/duplicate-aware graphs, Bayesian neighbor propagation, expected-F1 decisions, source-prior corrections, metadata representations and Other residual models. The old metric_graph “residual” parameter concatenates embedding coordinates; it is not C&S correction of anchored's OOF errors. I found no prior evaluation of the two specific anchored/C&S combinations above.

Cand4 is more different in its errors, but considerably weaker: 6 fixes versus 19 broken rows. Cand5 has only 3.25 fixes versus 14 broken rows and depends more on label-frequency adaptation. I would defer both as whole-model research branches.
